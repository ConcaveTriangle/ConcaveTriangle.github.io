# China - Continued

In July 2026, I returned to China again for 2 weeks. Whether I haven't experienced them a year earlier or simply forgot, here are some more observations / clarifications that did not make it into the first version. 

- Hotels and restaurants often emphasize luxury and quality through service. Room cleaning, hotel breakfast lounge, pool / gym amenities, restaurant waiters, etc. - all amazing. 
- Escorts are illegal, but there are sometimes certain... "services" in hotel massage parlors. Upon discovering this, I was not morally scandalized (escorts obviously exist in every country), but developed more of a mild disdain for the financial inefficiency (just get a girlfriend).
- Most people treat service workers with less respect/kindness than in the US, so a smile and being nice costs nothing and goes a long way. 
- Besides a phone, your 身份证 is one of the most important items to bring. It's used in everything from passing train/subway security to checking in at an internet cafe. 
- In particular, if you have a 身份证, it makes more sense to register with a Chinese cellular company (like 中国移动) than to use an international pass from your existing company (buy a small one and keep it as an emergency backup though). The prices are absurd - around 14 cents per GB. 
- Monsoon season (late summer to early fall) means that every day is cloudy and could rain. Keep an umbrella handy - it could go from nothing to pouring in 5 minutes. 
- Police or security are people too - they sometimes "睁一只眼，闭一只眼 [Look the other way]" so you can save face. If you have a date behind you on the scooter (technically prohibited), they usually won't write you a ticket if you're being safe. 
- On an electric scooter, the streets get considerably more fun at night - lot less people on the bike lane, and if you really want to push your bike, you can go on the main road (purely hypothetically, of course). 
- At intersections in every major city, the streetlights follow a deterministic pattern - (1) street A straight, (2) street A left turn, (3) street B straight, (4) street B left turn. You can exploit this to skip cycles legally (0.75 steps vs. 1.5 normally). WLOG, assume you are on street A, going straight:
  1. Trivial case; you simply go. 
  2. You turn left, wait for (3), and go straight; turn left once you crossed back across street A. 
  3. You cross street A, wait for (4), and turn left. 
  4. Just wait for one cycle. 
- China has almost the opposite sense of safety compared to the US. One would feel considerably less safe walking through a Minneapolis block than suburban Palo Alto, for instance. However, because of the density of cameras in Chinese cities (especially the capital), walking past midnight in Beijing feels much safer than through some rural Chinese agricultural town or greenway. 
- 万里木兰 at 霸王茶姬 is my favorite milk tea here (one of my favorite memories is buying 2 and drinking them through a Valorant session), although the 157mg of caffeine (yes, I memorized it because it's 50π) makes it situational. It's good in the morning, at internet cafes, while doing some competition math, or while flooring it down the road on a electric scooter (or so I've heard). It's bad if you need to be relaxed / calm, speak slow, or sleep directly after. 
- Internet cafe chains (网鱼电竞 in particular), are very commonplace, have locations in most major cities, and make more sense than buying your own setup. The lobby oftentimes have people smoking (although you can learn some wonderfully creative chinese slurs involving the recipient's bloodline), but the private booths barely cost more, are free to reserve for an arbitrary amount of time, and have amazing hardware. Where else are you getting a 7800x3d, 5080, and a 1440p 480hz OLED monitor for a dollar per hour?
- I got my physical done, and it was mesmerizingly efficient. After booking it on 美团 (think of it like Doordash, Yelp, and Expedia in one app) and checking in, the reception hands you a checklist of ~80 things to do, and a room to start. You then go through each room, with a separate professional (diploma on the wall). They check a box or two of the area they're supposed to check (i.e. cardiogram, MRI, blood draw, lungs, eyes, etc.) and give you the next room to go to. Once you filled up the checklist, you're usually hungry (as you're required to fast for 12 hours beforehand), so you go and get some food (included in the bill) at the lounge. You get your results online and, optionally, through mail. The whole elegant experience lasted around 3 hours and costed less than 150 dollars. 
- Trains are cheaper and a lot more comfortable than flights. Some examples being: 
  - Their processing is a lot faster than at airports - while you may need to arrive early an hour or two for a flight, you can arrive 15 minutes early for a train and be fine. Sometimes, stations even have workers that lead you directly to your track for exactly this situation. 
  - You can order cuisine from passing cities directly onto the train. This is significantly harder at 9km. 
  - There's much less noise / vibration. 
  - There's a stable cellular connection (sadly, the wi-fi was disabled during both of my trips, as they were in the middle of changing network companies). 
  - The acceleration feels much gentler (and there's no turbulence). 
  - Silently gliding by a countryside at 300 km/h feels elegant in a way that flights can't reproduce. 
- While the trains stay the same price no matter when you book the ticket, they are still first come first serve. In particular, first-class and business-class tickets at popular times tend to sell out. If you have to get somewhere, this is where waitlisting or booking a "superset" (a neat trick I made of getting a ticket that passes through your origin and destination) is useful. 
- While the subway may be faster. It can also be more crammed. Walking from the station to the destination in 40 degree weather is also not fun. Take a taxi if you want to arrive composed, such as for a formal dinner, date, or event. 
- The cultural slogans are surprisingly witty with wordplay, powerful, and morale-boosting, with statements from "宁停三分，不抢一秒 [Rather save a life than save a second]", "喝酒不开车，开车不喝酒 [Don't drink while driving, don't drive after drinking (this one sounds smoother in Chinese)"]", 人与自然和谐共生 [Live harmoniously with nature and treat it well]", to "贩毒是杀人，吸毒是自杀[Dealing drugs is murder, doing drugs is suicide]". 
- Just like how western news sometimes portrays China as a dangerous dictatorial orwellian police state, Chinese news portray America as a lawless wasteland where people shoot each other over the smallest disagreement (basically, Texas). Both sides exaggerate. At a dinner, I watched a relative confidently describe everyone in California, especially San Francisco, as gay. Now, I obviously know less about Cali than my relative, so apparently I learned something new about myself (bummer. I really thought I liked girls).
